Case for a rebuild

Lonely Light lies on the very fringe of George R.R. Martin’s world, but despite that it’s one of his most mysterious and interesting locations. The server’s current version has some major canon issues. For example, Great Wyk is currently more westerly than Lonely Light, and the entire island fits within the Riverrun walls. A complete rebuild would bring Lonely Light closer to the established lore and give it even more love.

Lonely Light would serve as a prototype/proof of concept for the rest of the Iron Islands. Its remote location makes it an ideal candidate for such a project, as a rebuild would have little to no impact on the surrounding areas.

Relevant Canon

House Farwynd of the Lonely Light is the senior branch of House Farwynd from the Iron Islands. Their seat is the Lonely Light, the main island in a group of thirteen islets eight days sail to the northwest of Great Wyk. Some of these islands are too small to support even a single household. There, seals and sea lions make their rookeries.

The main island, Lonely Light, is named after its beacon that shines day and night. In The World of Ice and Fire Maester Yandel tells us “the Lonely Light stands farthest west of all the lands known to us”. However, Archmaester Gyldayn writes in Fire & Blood that the Targaryen Islands discovered by Alys Westhill are even further west than Lonely Light, though those islands are all uninhabited.

A thousand years after Bradon the Shipwright sailed into the sunset sea and was never seen again, a group of ironborn sailors were blown off course from Great Wyk into a cluster of islands eight days sail from the closest shore. Their captain built a tower and a beacon there, took the name “Farwynd”, and declared the Lonely Light his seat.

The people of the Lonely Light are described as queer folk. They are rumoured to be skin changers who can turn into sea creatures, and to lie with seals in order to birth half-human children.

They have a cadet branch, House Farwynd of Sealskin Point, whose seat is on the north-westernmost lands of Great Wyk.

Their sigil is a black longship against a setting sun. Their words remain unknown, and they serve as bannermen to House Greyjoy of Pyke.

Economy

We get a good insight into the economy of Lonely Light in A Feast for Crows, “The Drowned Man”:

The offerings that his men spilled out before the kingsmoot included sealskins and walrus tusks, arm rings made of whalebone, warhorns banded in bronze. The captains looked and turned away, leaving lesser men to help themselves to the gifts.

These gifts were probably the finest goods the Farwynds could offer. It tells us that Farwynd is most likely a very poor house, even by ironborn standards. Their economy relies primarily on fishing, shepherding and the hunting of seals, walruses and whales. Whaling plays a particularly central role, providing not only food but also blubber for the oil that keeps the beacon ablaze day and night.

Much like the Norse settlers of the Faroe Islands and Iceland, the Farwynds would be dependent on trade to acquire otherwise unavailable resources, such as iron, bronze, grain for livestock and timber.

Like other ironborn, the Farwynds would supplement their economy through raiding. However, their remote location and small population would likely limit the scale and frequency of their raids.

Architecture

The architecture will be based on the houses excavated in Kvívík and Fuglafjørður: typical longhouses made of stone, with one room, a fireplace in the middle and benches along the walls. Because the islands have little to no trees, the structures will be made almost entirely of stone, turf and thatch.

Flora

Trees

The extreme oceanic climate, with winds whipping vast quantities of sea salt into the air, makes the islands very unfavourable to trees. Therefore the island will have no native forests. But, similar to Greenland and the Faroe Islands, some small and twisted trees will grow here in small populations. These trees rarely provide wood suitable for construction or larger objects, so the Farwynds would need to rely on driftwood or imported timber for construction, boatbuilding and the production of all manner of objects.

Society & Culture

Settlements

Due to Lonely Light’s remote location, the settlements would have no need to sit within defensive walls, as was common in parts of Scandinavia.

Instead I draw inspiration from Norse settlements in Greenland, the Faroe Islands, Iceland and North America.

The majority of Faroese villages (and towns) is located on the shore of the individual islands, there is virtually no inland settlement.

Most settlements will be situated along sheltered coastlines and natural harbours. Inland areas will be much more sparsely populated, consisting primarily of grazing land and isolated shepherds’ huts.

The north-western hamlet will take inspiration from this Faroese village and from this reconstructed Greenlandic Norse settlement.

The village on the southern island will be inspired by this North American settlement.

And by Velbastaður, on the Faroe Islands. I couldn’t find any reference images of how the village of Velbastaður looked, but I did find a very good 3D rendering of the area of archaeological remains. It gives a good insight into their settlement layout.

The main village will have a somewhat different layout. Its natural harbour will resemble a fjord, with elevated terrain rising on either side of the entrance, and the beacon on the highest point, overlooking the village below and marking the entrance to the harbour.

Rediscovery of Lonely Light

Bradon the Shipwright

The parallels between the story of Lonely Light’s discovery and the Icelandic sagas are too similar to be a coincidence.

ironborn sailing out of Great Wyk were blown off course to a cluster of islands eight days sail from the closest shore. Their captain built a tower and a beacon there, took the name “Farwynd”, and declared the Lonely Light his seat.
Naddod the Viking was sailing from Norway to the Faeroe Islands when he was blown off course and made landfall in Iceland’s Eastern Fjords.

When the Norse settlers arrived in Iceland, they discovered evidence that they were not the first people to inhabit the island. Irish monks had lived there before the arrival of the Vikings, but had abandoned their settlements long before.

I would like to incorporate a similar idea into the history of Lonely Light.

In my interpretation, some of the ships belonging to Bradon the Shipwright’s voyage may have been separated and landed on the islands. Rather than continuing westward, they could have settled there. Alternatively, Bradon himself first landed on the Lonely Light with his fleet before continuing westward into the Sunset Sea, leaving a small number of his men behind.

Over time, these early settlers may have either abandoned the islands or simply disappeared, leaving behind only traces of their presence: an old burial site built in the ways of the First Men and the ruins of a house.
